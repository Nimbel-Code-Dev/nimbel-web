# NewsUp — PR and corporate communications platform

**From a prototype that couldn't go into production to a PR platform with a dozen clients, several of them large companies.**

## The problem
In 2019, Gerard Gil realized that brands and communications agencies were still working the old way: press releases sent as PDFs by email, press contacts in Excel spreadsheets and tools that didn't talk to each other. Many didn't even use an email marketing tool. The press release reached the journalist and stopped there: there was no place to go back to and download the press kit or high-quality photos, and no way of knowing who had read it. Gerard looked for a product that solved all of it together and couldn't find one. There is competition today, but back then nothing like it existed. So he decided to build it himself.

## When we arrived
In summer 2023, after almost two years with two other providers, NewsUp came to Nimbel. The first one left the project a few months after starting. With the second, the team kept changing, with junior profiles rotating in and out, and Gerard didn't get the follow-up or the support he needed. When we reviewed the code, we found a prototype: no architecture, no design of its own and none of the basic security measures needed to go into production. It wasn't built to grow.

## How we did it
Gerard arrived wary. After two providers, he didn't want to start from scratch with a third: he wanted to see how we worked before trusting us. So we started with small jobs: fixing, one by one, the parts of the prototype that didn't work. As we solved them, we went a bit further and advised him on the product and on where to take the platform. With trust came the big changes. Piece by piece, we replaced the legacy code with a new architecture, until NewsUp went into production. Today nothing is left of the original prototype.

## What we did
A platform with a shared backend and three applications: the work dashboard, the public press rooms and the product website. It includes:
- **Digital press room** on each client's own domain: press releases, press kits and high-resolution media galleries.
- **Shared management between brand and agencies.** Each brand decides whether it runs its press room alone, with one agency or with several at once. Agencies join the brand's press room and send their press releases to their own contacts, without them getting mixed up: the agency keeps its list without the brand seeing it, and the brand can send to those contacts without seeing them. That way, the platform adapts to whatever agreement the brand and the agency reach.
- **Digital asset manager (DAM)** for high-quality documents, photos and videos. When a file is uploaded to a category, it is added to that category's image and video collections automatically: everything gets organized without anyone sorting it by hand.
- **Press release distribution** with a visual editor, scheduled and automated sends, and teasers that take the journalist to the press room.
- **Embargoed information.** The brand can send a press release to journalists before making anything public, so they can prepare their story before the curtain goes up. Before reading it, the journalist signs that they won't publish anything until a specific date and time. Access is protected by a token tied to their account and email: if they forward it to someone else, it won't open.
- **Press CRM** with tag-based segmentation and duplicate management.
- **Analytics** for opens, clicks and press room activity, with reports per campaign.
- Integrated payments and subscriptions.

## AI takes the idea further
NewsUp no longer only solves what Gerard saw in 2019. With artificial intelligence, the platform does many tasks for the user that used to be manual:
- It translates press releases directly into other languages.
- It describes images and finds similar ones.
- It categorizes files.
- It offers semantic search, which understands what you're looking for thanks to AI-generated descriptions.

The result is less work for brands, agencies and journalists, who find what they need to write their story sooner.
